A more general equation for a sinusoid:
$$

$$
$$
\text{Equ. (9.7)}\\
$$
$$
\begin{align}

    v(t) = V_{m}\sin(\omega t+\phi)
\end{align}
$$
where $(\omega + T)$ is the argument and $\phi$ is the *phase*
**both argument and phase can be in radians or degrees**

Looking at two equations
$\text{Equ. (9.8)}$

$$
v_{t}=\sin(\omega t) \text{ and } v_{2}=V_{m} \sin(\omega t+\phi)
$$

Shown in $\text{figure 9.2}$ $V_{2}$ occurs first in time. Therefore $V_{2}$ *leads* $V_{1}$ by $\phi$ or that $v_{1}$ *lags* $v_{2}$ by $\phi$ if $\phi \neq 0$ we also can say that $V_{1}$  and $V_{2}$ are *out of phase* if $\phi = 0$ they are *in phase*; they reach their min and max at the same time.
**They operate at the same frequency and do not have to have the same amplitude**
