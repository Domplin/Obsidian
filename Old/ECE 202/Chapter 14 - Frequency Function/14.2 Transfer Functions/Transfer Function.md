The **Transfer Function** $H(\omega)$ (also called the network function) is a useful tool for finding the frequency response of a circuit. 

A transfer function is the frequency-dependent ratio of a forced function to a forcing function (*output to input*) The idea of a transfer function was implicit when we used concepts of [[Impedance]] and [[Admittance]] relating to voltage and current.

Generally a linear network can be represented by the block diagram:![[Figure 14.1.png]]

The **Transfer Function** $H(\omega)$ of a circuit is the frequency-depended ration of [[Phasor]] output $Y(\omega)$ (an element voltage or current) to a [[Phasor]] input $X(\omega)$ (source voltage or current):
$$
H(\omega)=\frac{Y(\omega)}{X(\omega)} 
$$ 
Because its possible to to confuse them with the same symbols used for [[Reactance]] and [[Admittance]] they can be written as $H(j\omega)$ rather than $H(\omega)$ because $\omega$ and $j$ are an inseparable pair.

There are only four [[Possible Transfer Functions]] 

Being a complex quantity $H(\omega)$ has a magnitude $h(\omega)$ and a phase $\phi$; that is:
$$
H(\omega) = h(\omega)\angle\phi 
$$
